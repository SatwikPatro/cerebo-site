Privacy Policy

Cerebo · Last updated 25 November 2026

Cerebo collects no data about you. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and Cerebo has no servers. The app goes online on its own only to read a link you share with it or paste into the chat. What you create stays on your device. If your device is signed into iCloud, iCloud sync (which you can turn off in Settings) moves it through your own private iCloud account, and Apple Intelligence requests are processed privately on your device or by Apple's Private Cloud Compute. The developer cannot access any of it.

What Cerebo stores, and where

Your decks, cards, images, audio recordings, PDF attachments, study history and settings are stored locally on your device, inside the app's own container. Your chats with Cerebo are kept on the device you had them on, and are not synced. None of this is sent anywhere by the app, except as described under iCloud sync and Apple Intelligence below.

iCloud sync

iCloud sync is on by default when your device is signed into iCloud, and can be turned off at any time in Settings. While it is on, your decks and cards are synchronised between your own Apple devices using Apple's CloudKit, stored in your private CloudKit database.

  • The data lives in your personal iCloud account, not on any server the developer runs.
  • The developer has no access to it and cannot read it.
  • Apple's handling of that data is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • Turning sync off stops further synchronisation. Removing the data from iCloud is done through your device's iCloud settings.

Camera, photos and microphone

Cerebo asks for these only when you use the feature that needs them, and only to put content on a card:

  • Camera — to photograph or scan something onto a card.
  • Photo library — to add images you choose to a card.
  • Microphone — to record an audio clip onto a card, or a lecture to make cards from.

The resulting files are saved with your card on your device. Text recognition (OCR) on an image, the transcript of a recording and the text a video shows are made on your device. When you ask Apple Intelligence to make cards from a photo, scan or recording, what it reads from them is processed as described under Apple Intelligence below. You can decline any of these permissions and keep using the rest of the app.

Apple Intelligence

Cerebo's AI features (the chat, cards made from documents, photos and notes, study guides, practice tests and generated answers) use Apple Intelligence through Apple's FoundationModels framework. Depending on the choice in Settings → AI Text Generation, a request is processed on your device or by Apple's Private Cloud Compute:

  • Private Cloud (the default on iOS 27) sends requests to Private Cloud Compute, and uses your device when Private Cloud Compute can't answer, for example offline or past Apple's daily limit.
  • Hybrid sends heavy work, such as cards from a document, study guides and practice tests, to Private Cloud Compute, and handles quick tasks on your device.
  • On Device keeps every request on your device. On iOS 26, every request stays on your device.

Private Cloud Compute processes a request only to answer it. Apple does not keep it and does not make it accessible to Apple. Cerebo has no AI service of its own and no content telemetry, so the developer neither sees nor receives your prompts, notes or cards. The features appear only on devices that support Apple Intelligence, and Apple's terms govern the framework's behaviour. The support page lists which requests go where.

Sharing to Cerebo

What you send with Make Cards in Cerebo in the share sheet is copied into Cerebo's storage on your device, and stays there until you use it or discard it. Text from a Safari page is read in the page itself.

A link you share or paste into the chat is read by Cerebo straight from its site, or from that site's public interface for it, to make cards from the page or the PDF it points to. The site sees a request from your device, as it would from a browser, but no account or cookies go with it, and Cerebo keeps nothing of the visit. If the site asks you to prove you're human or to sign in, Open the Page shows it in Cerebo so you can, and a link there to another site opens in Safari. Cerebo never downloads a video: for YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and the like it reads only the title and description.

Translation and text-to-speech

The translate and read-aloud features use Apple's own system Translation and speech frameworks. These are presented by the operating system; the developer does not receive the text you translate or have read aloud. Apple may need to download language assets to your device to provide them.

Importing and exporting

Importing an Anki .apkg, CSV or JSON file reads that file directly from your device. Exporting writes a file you choose the destination for. Neither passes through any server.

Spotlight, widgets, Siri and Apple Watch

Cerebo indexes your cards with Spotlight so you can find them from your Home Screen, shares a small summary of what is due with its own widgets through an App Group, donates deck shortcuts to Siri, and sends your decks to the Cerebo Apple Watch app. All of this stays between your own devices and the operating system.

No third parties

Cerebo contains no third-party SDKs, no analytics or crash-reporting libraries, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind across apps or websites. Nothing about you is sold or shared, because nothing is collected.

Children

Cerebo is suitable for all ages and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy
